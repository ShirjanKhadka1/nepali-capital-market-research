# Nepal's capital market — structure guide

Read this before researching any single company or instrument. This guide
is a map: it tells you who does what. Specific sizes, counts, and returns
are NOT stated here without a source — add them, sourced, in the research
files that use them, never by copying this guide's generalities as data.

## Participants (who does what)

| Participant | Role in plain language |
|---|---|
| SEBON (Securities Board of Nepal) | Regulator: licensing, rules, oversight of the market |
| NEPSE (Nepal Stock Exchange) | Trading platform for listed securities |
| Clearing / settlement institutions | Make sure trades actually settle (money vs securities) |
| Brokers (stockbrokers) | Access point for most investors to trade on NEPSE |
| Merchant bankers / issue managers | Help issuers bring securities to market; manage issues |
| Issuers (listed companies, government, corporates) | Raise capital by issuing shares, bonds, or debentures |
| Mutual funds | Pooled investment vehicles, units held by investors |
| Investors (retail, institutional) | Buyers and holders of securities |
| Depository / registrar services | Record ownership, handle transfers and corporate actions |

## Instruments (what can be held)

- **Listed equities (NEPSE):** shares of listed companies, traded on NEPSE.
- **Government securities:** issued by the government, generally via auction
  and held/traded through the relevant channels.
- **Corporate bonds / debentures:** debt issued by companies, with interest
  and maturity terms stated in the issue documents.
- **Mutual fund units:** pooled funds, with a stated objective, manager,
  and fee structure in the fund documents.
- **Preference shares, rights, bonus shares:** corporate actions that change
  a holder's position — always check the issuer's announcement, not a summary.

## How to research any slice (repeatable method)

1. Name the instrument or participant precisely.
2. Find its primary document first: issue prospectus, fund document,
   SEBON/NEPSE publication, or issuer filing — not a news summary.
3. Extract: terms, size, dates, fees, risks — each with source + period.
4. Explain the mechanism in plain language: how does someone buy, hold,
   sell, or redeem this?
5. List what you could NOT verify, as TO VERIFY.

## Common traps

- **Ticker confusion:** same letters, different exchange, different company.
  Always state exchange + full company name.
- **Price without context:** a price move without volume, period, and
  corporate actions (bonus, rights, splits) is not an analysis.
- **News as source:** a news article reporting a figure is not the figure's
  source. Trace it to the filing or publication behind it.
- **"The market" as one thing:** equities, bonds, and funds do not move
  together and do not share the same risks. Name the slice.
